# Responsible Use & Ethical Governance Specification

## 1. System Intent: Decision Support, Not Automated Decision-Making
The **Fair Student-Support Prioritization System** is engineered strictly as an **educator-facing decision-support instrument**. 

- **Not an Automated Allocation Engine**: This system does not automatically enroll, penalize, track, or deny services to any student.
- **Not a Diagnostic Label**: A high "Support Need Score" is an indicator of statistical association with academic vulnerability based on historical data patterns; it is **not** a psychological evaluation, cognitive assessment, or permanent academic label.
- **Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Review**: All prioritizations generated by this system must serve as a preliminary agenda for multidisciplinary school teams (teachers, guidance counselors, academic advisors). Human educators must apply holistic, qualitative context (such as recent life events, undocumented health factors, or individual motivation) before finalizing any allocation of limited intervention slots.

---

## 2. Fairness-Through-Unawareness & Limitations of Demographic Blindness
To prevent direct disparate treatment:
- **Protected Attributes Excluded**: Demographic characteristics (`sex`) and institutional indicators (`school`) are **strictly barred from entering the model's feature vector**. The model has zero direct visibility into these variables during scoring or inference.
- **Fairness Auditing Only**: Protected attributes are collected and passed through purely for downstream demographic parity and recall auditing.
- **Known Limitation — Proxy Bias**: While demographic blindness prevents explicit discrimination, it does **not** guarantee the complete absence of indirect or proxy bias. Correlated features (such as family education level, study environment, or attendance patterns) can reflect systemic historical inequities. Continuous post-deployment monitoring across diverse demographic cohorts is essential.

---

## 3. The Rejected Post-Hoc Fairness Correction Experiment
During the development and benchmark validation phases, we rigorously tested a post-hoc group-level fairness mitigation technique:

### The Experiment
- **Hypothesis**: By learning an additive group log-odds adjustment $\Delta_g$ on a nested tuning split of the training data to boost under-selected groups prior to ranking, we could mechanically compress the group recall gap.
- **In-Sample Tuning Observation**: On the internal tuning split, the post-hoc log-odds shift appeared highly effective, shrinking the observed recall gap from **0.34 down to 0.06**.

### Why It Was Rejected
When evaluated with proper scientific rigor on genuinely held-out data across 10 random seeds, the intervention failed to generalize:
1. **Zero Out-of-Sample Selection Benefit**: On held-out validation data, the learned shift produced negligible to zero change in actual student selection.
2. **Degraded Probabilistic Calibration**: The uncalibrated score shifting degraded probability calibration, worsening the mean Brier score from **0.065 to 0.074**.
3. **Statistically Ineffective Over 10 Seeds**: Across 10 independent random train/validation splits, the mean recall gap was virtually identical (**0.220 ± 0.106 baseline vs. 0.208 ± 0.112 shifted**), well within standard error.

### Governance Decision
We made the deliberate engineering decision to **reject artificial post-hoc score distortion and deploy the plain, uncorrected, well-calibrated Logistic Regression model**. In high-stakes educational resource allocation, honest probability estimates and transparent ranking are far superior to fragile in-sample patches that degrade calibration without providing genuine equity gains on new cohorts.

## 4. Plain-Language, Non-Causal Explainability
Explanations provided by this system follow strict communication guardrails:
- **No Causal Verbs**: Explanations never claim that a factor "caused", "forced", or "was responsible for" a student's performance.
- **Directional Precision**: Features are mapped with factual accuracy based on actual values (e.g. `Pstatus=T` accurately reflects parents living together, rather than an assumed default).
- **Educational Context**: Factors are presented as "statistical associations identified by the model" to help counselors ask informed, empathetic questions during student check-ins.

---

## 5. Deployment Checklist for School Districts
Prior to utilizing this system in operational school settings:
1. **Audit Local Cohort Representation**: Verify that the student population resembles the training distribution and evaluate group sample sizes.
2. **Review Group Eligibility**: Confirm that cohorts contain sufficient sample sizes ($\ge 10$ students, $\ge 3$ historical support needs) to avoid statistical noise in fairness reporting.
3. **Counselor Training**: Ensure participating educators understand that the 0–100 Need Score represents relative priority under fixed capacity constraints, not an absolute measure of student potential.
